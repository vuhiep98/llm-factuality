# An Analysis of Multilingual FActScore

[![License: MIT](https://img.shields.io/badge/License-MIT-green.svg)](https://opensource.org/licenses/MIT)

Code for [An Analysis of Multilingual FActScore](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.247.pdf), by
Vu Trong Kim,  Michael Krumdick, Varshini Reddy, Franck Dernoncourt, and  Viet Dac Lai. Our implementation is based on [FActScore](https://github.com/shmsw25/FActScore) and [long-form-factuality](https://github.com/google-deepmind/long-form-factuality/)

## **Overview**
FActScore has gained popularity as a metric to estimate the factuality of long-form texts generated by Large Language Models (LLMs) in English. However, there has not been any work in studying the behavior of FActScore in other languages. This paper studies the limitations of each component in the fourcomponent pipeline of FActScore in the multilingual setting. We introduce a new dataset for FActScore on texts generated by strong multilingual LLMs. Our evaluation shows that LLMs exhibit distinct behaviors in both fact extraction and fact scoring tasks. No LLM produces consistent and reliable FActScore across languages with varying levels of resources. We also find that the knowledge source plays an important role in the quality of the estimated FActScore. Using Wikipedia as the knowledge source may hinder the true FActScore of longform text due to its limited coverage in medium and low-resource languages. We also incorporate three mitigations to our knowledge source that ultimately improve FActScore estimation across all languages.

## **Data**
We're proud to share all of our newly annotated data by native speakers of three languages (Spanish, Arabic and Bengali)! All data is organized in json or jsonl format, for your pleasure :)

Cloud storage folder link: [Google Drive](https://drive.google.com/drive/folders/1k5HRukoJgugwM8juyJ4RtP9qC8BgXyBi?usp=sharing)
### File List
- `annotation_wiki_all.zip`: annotations in three studied languages using Wikipedia (i.e., es.wikipedia, ar.wikipedia, bn.wikipedia) as the knowledge source.
- `annotation_w_different_source.zip`: annotations in three studied languages using target language Wikipedia, English Wikipedia and the Internet as knowledge sources.
